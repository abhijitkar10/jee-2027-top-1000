### C-ION-01 · B — JEE Main 2026 · 21 Jan Shift 2 · set Q1 · Numerical

The first and second ionisation constants of $\mathrm{H_2X}$ are $2.5\times10^{-8}$ and $1.0\times10^{-13}$ respectively. The concentration of $\mathrm{X^{2-}}$ in $0.1\ \mathrm M\ \mathrm{H_2X}$ solution is ______ $\times10^{-15}$ M. (Nearest integer)

**Answer: 100** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** First ionisation dominates: $[\mathrm{H^+}]=[\mathrm{HX^-}]=\sqrt{K_{a1}C}=\sqrt{2.5\times10^{-8}\times0.1}=5\times10^{-5}$ M. Then $K_{a2}=\dfrac{[\mathrm{H^+}][\mathrm{X^{2-}}]}{[\mathrm{HX^-}]}$ with $[\mathrm{H^+}]\approx[\mathrm{HX^-}]$ gives $[\mathrm{X^{2-}}]\approx K_{a2}=1.0\times10^{-13}$ M $=100\times10^{-15}$ M.

### C-ION-02 · B — JEE Main 2026 · 23 Jan Shift 1 · set Q2 · Numerical

$x$ mg of pure HCl was used to make an aqueous solution. 25.0 mL of 0.1 M $\mathrm{Ba(OH)_2}$ solution is used when the HCl solution was titrated against it. The numerical value of $x$ is ______ $\times10^{-1}$. (Nearest integer) Given: molar mass of HCl and $\mathrm{Ba(OH)_2}$ are $36.5$ and $171.0$ g mol$^{-1}$ respectively.

**Answer: 1825** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** $\mathrm{Ba(OH)_2+2HCl\to BaCl_2+2H_2O}$. $n(\mathrm{Ba(OH)_2})=0.1\times0.025=2.5\times10^{-3}$ mol, so $n(\mathrm{HCl})=5\times10^{-3}$ mol, which is $5\times10^{-3}\times36.5=0.1825$ g $=182.5$ mg $=1825\times10^{-1}$ mg. So $x=1825$.

### C-ION-03 · B — JEE Main 2026 · 22 Jan Shift 2 · set Q3 · MCQ

Which of the following mixtures gives a buffer solution with $\mathrm{pH}=9.25$? Given: $\mathrm{p}K_b(\mathrm{NH_4OH})=4.75$.

(1) 0.2 M $\mathrm{NH_4OH}$ (0.5 L) + 0.1 M HCl (0.5 L)  (2) 0.2 M $\mathrm{NH_4OH}$ (0.4 L) + 0.1 M HCl (1 L)  (3) 0.4 M $\mathrm{NH_4OH}$ (1 L) + 0.1 M HCl (1 L)  (4) 0.5 M $\mathrm{NH_4OH}$ (0.2 L) + 0.2 M HCl (0.5 L)

**Answer: (1)** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** $\mathrm{pOH}=\mathrm{p}K_b+\log\dfrac{[\text{salt}]}{[\text{base}]}$, and $\mathrm{pH}=9.25\Rightarrow\mathrm{pOH}=4.75=\mathrm{p}K_b$, so $[\text{salt}]=[\text{base}]$ (half-neutralised). (1): 100 mmol base + 50 mmol HCl leaves 50 mmol base and makes 50 mmol salt – equal. (2): 80 mmol base, 100 mmol HCl – excess acid, no buffer. (3): 400 mmol base, 100 mmol HCl gives 300 : 100 – not equal. (4): 100 mmol each – only salt. So (1).

### C-ION-04 · B — JEE Main 2026 · 28 Jan Shift 1 · set Q4 · MCQ

Consider a weak base B of $\mathrm{p}K_b=5.699$. $x$ mL of 0.02 M HCl and $y$ mL of 0.02 M weak base B are mixed to make 100 mL of a buffer of pH 9 at $25^\circ$C. The values of $x$ and $y$ respectively are: [Given: $\log2=0.3010$, $\log3=0.4771$, $\log5=0.699$]

(1) $x=85.7$, $y=14.3$  (2) $x=14.3$, $y=85.7$  (3) $x=11.1$, $y=88.9$  (4) $x=42.7$, $y=57.3$

**Answer: (2)** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** $\mathrm{pOH}=14-9=5=\mathrm{p}K_b+\log\dfrac{[\mathrm{BH^+}]}{[\mathrm B]}\Rightarrow\log\dfrac{[\mathrm{BH^+}]}{[\mathrm B]}=-0.699\Rightarrow\dfrac{[\mathrm{BH^+}]}{[\mathrm B]}=\dfrac15$. HCl converts $x$ (in units of 0.02 mmol/mL) of B into $\mathrm{BH^+}$, leaving $y-x$ of B: $\dfrac{x}{y-x}=\dfrac15\Rightarrow y=6x$. With $x+y=100$: $x=\dfrac{100}{7}=14.3$ mL and $y=85.7$ mL.

### C-ION-05 · B — JEE Main 2026 · 28 Jan Shift 1 · set Q5 · Numerical

Consider the dissociation equilibrium of the weak acid $\mathrm{HA\rightleftharpoons H^+(aq)+A^-(aq)}$. If the $\mathrm{p}K_a$ of the acid is 4, then the pH of a 10 mM HA solution is ______ (nearest integer). [Given: the degree of dissociation can be neglected with respect to unity.]

**Answer: 3** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** $K_a=10^{-4}$, $C=10\ \mathrm{mM}=10^{-2}$ M. With $\alpha\ll1$: $[\mathrm{H^+}]=\sqrt{K_aC}=\sqrt{10^{-6}}=10^{-3}$ M, so $\mathrm{pH}=3$.

### C-ION-06 · B — JEE Main 2026 · 24 Jan Shift 1 · set Q6 · Numerical

Consider two Group IV metal ions $\mathrm{X^{2+}}$ and $\mathrm{Y^{2+}}$. A solution containing 0.01 M $\mathrm{X^{2+}}$ and 0.01 M $\mathrm{Y^{2+}}$ is saturated with $\mathrm{H_2S}$. The pH at which the metal sulphide YS will form as a precipitate is ______ (nearest integer). (Given: $K_{sp}(\mathrm{XS})=1\times10^{-22}$ at $25^\circ$C, $K_{sp}(\mathrm{YS})=4\times10^{-16}$ at $25^\circ$C, $[\mathrm{H_2S}]=0.1$ M in solution, $K_{a1}\times K_{a2}(\mathrm{H_2S})=1.0\times10^{-21}$, $\log2=0.30$, $\log3=0.48$, $\log5=0.70$)

**Answer: 4** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** YS precipitates when $[\mathrm{Y^{2+}}][\mathrm{S^{2-}}]\ge K_{sp}$: $[\mathrm{S^{2-}}]\ge\dfrac{4\times10^{-16}}{0.01}=4\times10^{-14}$ M. For saturated $\mathrm{H_2S}$, $[\mathrm{S^{2-}}]=\dfrac{K_{a1}K_{a2}[\mathrm{H_2S}]}{[\mathrm{H^+}]^2}=\dfrac{10^{-22}}{[\mathrm{H^+}]^2}$. So $[\mathrm{H^+}]^2\le\dfrac{10^{-22}}{4\times10^{-14}}=2.5\times10^{-9}$, $[\mathrm{H^+}]\le5\times10^{-5}$ M, i.e. $\mathrm{pH}\ge4.3\approx4$.

### C-ION-07 · C — JEE Advanced 2025 · Paper 1 · Q9 · Numerical

At $25^\circ$C, the concentration of $\text{H}^+$ in a $1.00\times10^{-3}$ M aqueous solution of a weak monobasic acid with $K_a=4.00\times10^{-11}$ is $X\times10^{-7}$ M. $X=$ ______. ($K_w=1.00\times10^{-14}$)

**Answer: ≈ 2.24** (accepted range $2.2$–$2.3$) · official key ✔ (re-derived)

**Solution.** The acid is so weak that water's own ionisation cannot be ignored. Charge balance: $[\text{H}^+]=[\text{A}^-]+[\text{OH}^-]$, with $[\text{A}^-]=\dfrac{K_aC}{[\text{H}^+]}$ and $[\text{OH}^-]=\dfrac{K_w}{[\text{H}^+]}$. So $[\text{H}^+]^2=K_aC+K_w=4\times10^{-14}+1\times10^{-14}=5\times10^{-14}$, giving $[\text{H}^+]=2.24\times10^{-7}$ M.

### C-ION-08 · C — JEE Main 2025 · Jan session · Ion set Q1 · Numerical

The total number of non-bonded electrons present in the NO$_2^-$ ion based on Lewis theory is ______.

**Answer: 12** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** NO$_2^-$ has $5+2\times6+1=18$ valence electrons. In the Lewis structure O=N–O$^-$ there are 3 bonding pairs (6 electrons: one double and one single N–O bond), so the non-bonded electrons are $18-6=12$ (one lone pair on N, two on the doubly bonded O and three on the singly bonded O$^-$).

### C-ION-09 · C — JEE Main 2025 · Jan session · Ion set Q2 · MCQ

A weak acid HA has degree of dissociation $x$. Which option gives the correct expression of (pH $-$ p$K_a$)?

(1) 0  (2) $\log(1+2x)$  (3) $\log\left(\dfrac{1-x}x\right)$  (4) $\log\left(\dfrac x{1-x}\right)$

**Answer: (4)** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** Henderson–Hasselbalch: pH $=$ p$K_a+\log\dfrac{[A^-]}{[HA]}$ with $[A^-]=Cx$ and $[HA]=C(1-x)$. So pH $-$ p$K_a=\log\dfrac x{1-x}$.

### C-ION-10 · C — JEE Main 2025 · Apr session · Ion set Q1 · Numerical

$x$ mg of Mg(OH)$_2$ (molar mass 58) is required to be dissolved in 1.0 L of water to produce a pH of 10.0 at 298 K. The value of $x$ is ______ mg. (Nearest integer) (Mg(OH)$_2$ is assumed to dissociate completely in H$_2$O.)

**Answer: 3** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** pH 10 means pOH 4 and $[OH^-]=10^{-4}$ M. Each Mg(OH)$_2$ gives 2OH$^-$, so $[Mg(OH)_2]=5\times10^{-5}$ M $=5\times10^{-5}\times58$ g/L $=2.9$ mg $\approx3$ mg.

### C-ION-12 · C — JEE Main 2024 · 27 Jan Shift 1 · Q35 · MCQ

Statement I: An aqueous solution of ammonium carbonate is basic.  
Statement II: The acidic/basic nature of the salt of a weak acid and a weak base depends on the $K_a$ and $K_b$ values of the acid and the base forming it.

(1) Both statements are correct  (2) I correct, II incorrect  (3) Both incorrect  (4) I incorrect, II correct

**Answer: (1)** · key ✔

**Solution.** For a weak-acid/weak-base salt, $\text{pH}=7+\tfrac12(\text{p}K_a-\text{p}K_b)$, so II is true. In $(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{CO}_3$, $K_b(\text{NH}_3)\approx1.8\times10^{-5}$ exceeds $K_a(\text{HCO}_3^-)\approx4.7\times10^{-11}$ (the acid conjugate to $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$), so carbonate hydrolyses more and the solution is basic. I is true.

### C-ION-13 · C — JEE Main 2024 · 30 Jan Shift 1 · Q55 · Numerical

The pH at which $\text{Mg(OH)}_2$ [$K_{sp}=1\times10^{-11}$] begins to precipitate from a solution containing $0.10$ M $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions is ______.

**Answer: 9** · key ✔

**Solution.** Precipitation begins when the ionic product equals $K_{sp}$: $[\text{OH}^-]^2=\dfrac{10^{-11}}{0.10}=10^{-10}\Rightarrow[\text{OH}^-]=10^{-5}$ M. $\text{pOH}=5$ so $\text{pH}=9$.

### C-ION-14 · C — JEE Main 2024 · 30 Jan Shift 2 · Q53 · Numerical

The pH of an aqueous solution containing $1$ M benzoic acid ($\text{p}K_a=4.20$) and $1$ M sodium benzoate is $4.5$. The volume of benzoic acid solution in $300$ mL of this buffer solution is ______ mL.

**Answer: 100** · key ✔

**Solution.** Henderson–Hasselbalch: $4.5=4.20+\log\dfrac{V_{\text{salt}}}{V_{\text{acid}}}\Rightarrow\dfrac{V_{\text{salt}}}{V_{\text{acid}}}=10^{0.3}=2$. Since both solutions are $1$ M, the mole ratio equals the volume ratio, and $V_{\text{acid}}+V_{\text{salt}}=300$ gives $V_{\text{acid}}=100$ mL.

### C-ION-15 · C — JEE Main 2024 · 1 Feb Shift 1 · Q38 · MCQ

Statement I: Potassium hydrogen phthalate is a primary standard for standardisation of sodium hydroxide solution.  
Statement II: In this titration phenolphthalein can be used as indicator.

(1) Both true  (2) I true, II false  (3) I false, II true  (4) Both false

**Answer: (1)** · key ✔

**Solution.** KHP is pure, non-hygroscopic and stable, so it is a primary standard for NaOH. The titration is weak acid–strong base, with an equivalence point in the basic region (pH $\approx 8.5$–$9$), which suits phenolphthalein (range $8.3$–$10$).

### C-ION-16 · C — JEE Main 2023 · 24 Jan Shift 1 · Q53 · Numerical

The dissociation constant of acetic acid is $x\times10^{-5}$. When $25$ mL of $0.2$ M $\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}$ solution is mixed with $25$ mL of $0.02$ M $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ solution, the pH of the resulting solution is $5$. $x=$ ______.

**Answer: 10** · key ✔

**Solution.** After mixing (volume doubled): $[\text{salt}]=0.1$ M and $[\text{acid}]=0.01$ M. Henderson: $\text{pH}=\text{p}K_a+\log\dfrac{0.1}{0.01}=\text{p}K_a+1=5\Rightarrow\text{p}K_a=4$, so $K_a=10^{-4}=10\times10^{-5}$, $x=10$.
