### C-PRO-01 — JEE Main 2024 · 27 Jan Shift 2 · Q36 · MCQ

The technique used for purification of a steam-volatile, water-immiscible substance is

(1) Fractional distillation  (2) Fractional distillation under reduced pressure  (3) Distillation  (4) Steam distillation

**Answer: (4)** · key ✔

**Solution.** In steam distillation the liquid boils when the sum of its vapour pressure and that of water equals atmospheric pressure, so it distils below $100^\circ$C without decomposition. Fractional distillation separates miscible liquids with close boiling points; reduced pressure is for substances that decompose near their boiling points.

### C-PRO-02 — JEE Main 2024 · 27 Jan Shift 2 · Q47 · MCQ

A phenolic group can be identified by a positive

(1) Phthalein dye test  (2) Lucas test  (3) Tollens' test  (4) Carbylamine test

**Answer: (1)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Phenol heated with phthalic anhydride and conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, then made alkaline, gives phenolphthalein (pink). Lucas tests alcohols, Tollens' tests aldehydes, and the carbylamine test detects primary amines.

### C-PRO-03 — JEE Main 2024 · 29 Jan Shift 1 · Q39 · MCQ

Appearance of a blood-red colour on treating the sodium fusion extract of an organic compound with $\text{FeSO}_4$ in the presence of conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ indicates the presence of the element(s)

(1) Br  (2) N  (3) N and S  (4) S

**Answer: (3)** · key ✔

**Solution.** In Lassaigne's test, sodium fusion converts N and S to $\text{NaSCN}$ when both are present. $\text{SCN}^-$ then reacts with $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ (from air oxidation of $\text{Fe}^{2+}$) to give the blood-red $[\text{Fe(SCN)}]^{2+}$. N alone gives Prussian blue and S alone gives a violet colour with nitroprusside.

### C-PRO-04 — JEE Main 2024 · 29 Jan Shift 2 · Q38 · MCQ

Chromatographic technique(s) based on the principle of differential adsorption is/are:
A. Column chromatography  B. Thin layer chromatography  C. Paper chromatography

(1) B only  (2) A only  (3) A and B only  (4) C only

**Answer: (3)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Column and thin-layer chromatography use a solid adsorbent (alumina/silica gel) and separate by differing adsorption of components. Paper chromatography is a partition technique between water held in the cellulose and the mobile phase.

### C-PRO-05 — JEE Main 2024 · 30 Jan Shift 1 · Q49 · MCQ

The Lassaigne's extract is boiled with dil. $\text{HNO}_3$ before testing for halogens because

(1) AgCN is soluble in $\text{HNO}_3$  (2) Silver halides are soluble in $\text{HNO}_3$  (3) $\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$ is soluble in $\text{HNO}_3$  (4) $\text{Na}_2\text{S}$ and NaCN are decomposed by $\text{HNO}_3$

**Answer: (4)** · key ✔

**Solution.** If present, $\text{S}^{2-}$ and $\text{CN}^-$ would also precipitate with $\text{AgNO}_3$ and interfere. Boiling with $\text{HNO}_3$ destroys them: $\text{Na}_2\text{S}+2\text{HNO}_3\to2\text{NaNO}_3+\text{H}_2\text{S}\uparrow$ and $\text{NaCN}+\text{HNO}_3\to\text{NaNO}_3+\text{HCN}\uparrow$.

### C-PRO-06 — JEE Main 2024 · 30 Jan Shift 1 · Q57 · Numerical

On a thin layer chromatography plate an organic compound moved by $3.5$ cm while the solvent moved by $5$ cm. The retardation factor of the organic compound is ______ $\times10^{-1}$.

**Answer: 7** · key ✔

**Solution.** $R_f=\dfrac{\text{distance moved by compound}}{\text{distance moved by solvent}}=\dfrac{3.5}{5}=0.7=7\times10^{-1}$.

### C-PRO-07 — JEE Main 2024 · 30 Jan Shift 2 · Q35 · MCQ

Which among the following purification methods is based on the principle of "solubility" in two different solvents?

(1) Column chromatography  (2) Sublimation  (3) Distillation  (4) Differential extraction

**Answer: (4)** · key ✔

**Solution.** In differential (solvent) extraction an organic compound is transferred from an aqueous solution into an immiscible organic solvent in which it is more soluble, using the distribution law. Chromatography uses adsorption/partition, sublimation uses vapour pressure and distillation uses boiling point differences.

### C-PRO-08 — JEE Main 2024 · 31 Jan Shift 1 · Q36 · MCQ

The "adsorption" principle is used for which of the following purification methods?

(1) Extraction  (2) Chromatography  (3) Distillation  (4) Sublimation

**Answer: (2)** · key ✔

**Solution.** In chromatography (column, TLC) components separate by differing adsorption on a stationary phase. Extraction uses solubility, distillation boiling points, sublimation vapour pressure.

### C-PRO-09 — JEE Main 2024 · 31 Jan Shift 1 · Q38 · MCQ

Match List I (technique) with List II (application): A. Distillation  B. Fractional distillation  C. Steam distillation  D. Distillation under reduced pressure; I. Separation of glycerol from spent lye  II. Aniline–water mixture  III. Separation of crude oil fractions  IV. Chloroform–aniline.

(1) A-IV, B-I, C-II, D-III  (2) A-IV, B-III, C-II, D-I  (3) A-I, B-II, C-IV, D-III  (4) A-II, B-III, C-I, D-IV

**Answer: (2)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Simple distillation separates liquids with a large boiling-point gap (chloroform–aniline). Fractional distillation separates crude-oil fractions. Steam distillation suits water-immiscible, steam-volatile aniline. Reduced-pressure distillation is used for glycerol (decomposes near its normal boiling point).

### C-PRO-10 — JEE Main 2024 · 31 Jan Shift 2 · Q39 · MCQ

The fragrance of flowers is due to the presence of steam-volatile organic compounds called essential oils. They are generally insoluble in water at room temperature but miscible with water vapour in the vapour phase. A suitable method for the extraction of these oils from the flowers is

(1) crystallisation  (2) distillation under reduced pressure  (3) distillation  (4) steam distillation

**Answer: (4)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Steam distillation suits substances that are volatile in steam, immiscible with water and would decompose at their normal boiling points. The oil co-distils with steam below $100^\circ$C and is separated from the condensed water.

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 23 Jan Shift 1 · Analysis set Q4 · MCQ

Given below are two statements:

Statement I: Sublimation is used for the separation and purification of compounds with a low melting point.
Statement II: The boiling point of a liquid increases as the external pressure is reduced.

(1) Both Statement I and Statement II are true  (2) Statement I is true but Statement II is false  (3) Both Statement I and Statement II are false  (4) Statement I is false but Statement II is true

**Answer: (3)** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** Sublimation purifies solids that pass directly into vapour on heating (e.g. naphthalene, camphor, benzoic acid) – not compounds characterised by a low melting point; I false. A liquid boils when its vapour pressure equals the external pressure, so *lowering* the external pressure *lowers* the boiling point (the basis of distillation under reduced pressure); II false.

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 24 Jan Shift 2 · Analysis set Q8 · Numerical

0.25 g of an organic compound A containing carbon, hydrogen and oxygen was analysed by the combustion method. The mass of the $\mathrm{CaCl_2}$ tube and the potash tube increased by 0.15 g and 0.1837 g, respectively. The percentage of oxygen in compound A is ______ % (nearest integer). (Molar mass in g mol$^{-1}$: H 1, C 12, O 16)

**Answer: 73** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** $\mathrm{H_2O}=0.15$ g gives $\mathrm H=\dfrac{2}{18}\times0.15=0.01667$ g (6.67%); $\mathrm{CO_2}=0.1837$ g gives $\mathrm C=\dfrac{12}{44}\times0.1837=0.0501$ g (20.04%). $\%\mathrm O=100-20.04-6.67=73.3\approx73$.

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 22 Jan Shift 2 · Analysis set Q10 · MCQ

When 1 g of compound X is subjected to Kjeldahl's method for estimation of nitrogen, 15 mL of 1 M $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ was neutralised by the ammonia evolved. The percentage of nitrogen in compound X is:

(1) 0.21  (2) 21  (3) 42  (4) 0.42

**Answer: (3)** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** $\mathrm{2NH_3+H_2SO_4\to(NH_4)_2SO_4}$: moles of $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}=0.015$, moles of $\mathrm{NH_3}=0.030$, so N $=0.030\times14=0.42$ g in 1 g: **42%**.

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 22 Jan Shift 1 · Analysis set Q11 · Numerical

The sodium fusion extract of an organic compound Y with $\mathrm{CHCl_3}$ and chlorine water gives a violet colour to the $\mathrm{CHCl_3}$ layer. 0.15 g of Y gave 0.12 g of the silver halide precipitate in the Carius method. The percentage of halogen in compound Y is ______ % (nearest integer). (Given: molar mass in g mol$^{-1}$: C 12, H 1, Cl 35.5, Br 80, I 127)

**Answer: 43** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** A violet $\mathrm{CHCl_3}$ layer with $\mathrm{Cl_2}$ water indicates iodine, so the precipitate is AgI ($M=108+127=235$). $\%\mathrm I=\dfrac{127}{235}\times\dfrac{0.12}{0.15}\times100=43.2\approx43$.

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 28 Jan Shift 1 · Analysis set Q18 · Numerical

0.53 g of an organic compound (x), heated with excess concentrated nitric acid and then with silver nitrate, gave 0.75 g of silver bromide. 1.0 g of (x) gave 1.32 g of $\mathrm{CO_2}$ on combustion. The percentage of hydrogen in the compound (x) is ______ % (nearest integer). [Molar mass in g mol$^{-1}$: H 1, C 12, Br 80, Ag 108, O 16; compound (x): $\mathrm{C_xH_yBr_z}$]

**Answer: 4** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** $\%\mathrm{Br}=\dfrac{80}{188}\times\dfrac{0.75}{0.53}\times100=60.2$. $\%\mathrm C=\dfrac{12}{44}\times\dfrac{1.32}{1.0}\times100=36.0$. The compound contains only C, H and Br, so $\%\mathrm H=100-60.2-36.0=3.8\approx4$ (it is $\mathrm{C_4H_5Br}$, $M=133$, $\%\mathrm H=3.76$).

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 24 Jan Shift 1 · Analysis set Q21 · Numerical

In the Dumas method for estimation of nitrogen, 0.50 g of an organic compound gave 70 mL of nitrogen collected at 300 K and 715 mm pressure. The percentage of nitrogen in the organic compound is ______ %. (Aqueous tension at 300 K is 15 mm.)

**Answer: 15** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** Pressure of dry $\mathrm N_2=715-15=700$ mm. Volume at STP: $V_0=\dfrac{700\times70\times273}{760\times300}=58.7$ mL. Mass of $\mathrm N_2=\dfrac{58.7}{22400}\times28=0.0734$ g, so $\%\mathrm N=\dfrac{0.0734}{0.50}\times100=14.7\approx15$.
