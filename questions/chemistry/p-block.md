### C-PBL-01 — JEE Main 2024 · 27 Jan Shift 1 · Q36 · MCQ

Assertion (A): The melting point of boron is unusually high among Group 13 elements.
Reason (R): Solid boron has a very strong crystalline lattice.

(1) Both true, R is not the correct explanation  (2) Both true, R is the correct explanation  (3) A true, R false  (4) A false, R true

**Answer: (2)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Boron exists as a covalent network of $\text{B}_{12}$ icosahedral units, so a very strong lattice must be broken to melt it (m.p. about 2453 K). This explains the anomalously high melting point.

### C-PBL-02 — JEE Main 2024 · 27 Jan Shift 1 · Q43 · MCQ

The element not showing a variable oxidation state is

(1) Bromine  (2) Iodine  (3) Chlorine  (4) Fluorine

**Answer: (4)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Fluorine is the most electronegative element and has no vacant d-orbitals, so it shows only $-1$ (besides 0). Cl, Br and I show $+1,+3,+5,+7$.

### C-PBL-03 — JEE Main 2024 · 27 Jan Shift 2 · Q40 · MCQ

Statement I: Oxygen, the first member of group 16, exhibits only the $-2$ oxidation state.
Statement II: Down group 16 the stability of the $+4$ oxidation state decreases and that of the $+6$ oxidation state increases.

(1) I correct, II incorrect  (2) Both correct  (3) Both incorrect  (4) I incorrect, II correct

**Answer: (3)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Oxygen also shows $-1$ (peroxides), $-\tfrac12$ (superoxides), $-\tfrac13$ (ozonide) and $+2$ (in $\text{OF}_2$), so I is wrong. Because of the inert-pair effect, down the group the $+4$ state becomes more stable and $+6$ less stable, the reverse of II, so II is wrong.

### C-PBL-04 — JEE Main 2024 · 27 Jan Shift 2 · Q53 · Numerical

$1$ mole of $\text{PbS}$ is oxidised by $X$ moles of $\text{O}_3$ to give $Y$ moles of $\text{O}_2$. Then $X+Y=$ ______.

**Answer: 8** · key ✔

**Solution.** $\text{PbS}+4\text{O}_3\to\text{PbSO}_4+4\text{O}_2$. So $X=4$, $Y=4$ and $X+Y=8$.

### C-PBL-05 — JEE Main 2024 · 29 Jan Shift 1 · Q36 · MCQ

Statement I: The electronegativity of group 14 elements from Si to Pb gradually decreases.
Statement II: Group 14 contains non-metallic, metallic as well as metalloid elements.

(1) I false, II true  (2) I true, II false  (3) Both true  (4) Both false

**Answer: (1)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Electronegativity is nearly constant from Si to Pb (Pauling $\approx1.8,1.8,1.8,1.9$), owing to poor shielding by $d$ and $f$ electrons, so I is false. Group 14 has non-metals (C), metalloids (Si, Ge) and metals (Sn, Pb), so II is true.

### C-PBL-06 — JEE Main 2024 · 29 Jan Shift 2 · Q39 · MCQ

The anomalous behaviour of oxygen is due to its

(1) large size and high electronegativity  (2) small size and low electronegativity  (3) small size and high electronegativity  (4) large size and low electronegativity

**Answer: (3)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Oxygen differs from the rest of group 16 (no $d$-orbitals, forms strong H-bonds, only $\text{O}=\text{O}$ multiple bonds) because of its small atomic size and high electronegativity.

### C-PBL-07 — JEE Main 2024 · 30 Jan Shift 1 · Q44 · MCQ

Aluminium chloride in acidified aqueous solution forms an ion having the geometry

(1) Octahedral  (2) Square planar  (3) Tetrahedral  (4) Trigonal bipyramidal

**Answer: (1)** · key ✔

**Solution.** $\text{Al}^{3+}$ is strongly hydrated: $\text{AlCl}_3+6\text{H}_2\text{O}\to[\text{Al(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{3+}+3\text{Cl}^-$. The hexaaqua ion is octahedral ($sp^3d^2$/$d^2sp^3$-type description).

### C-PBL-08 — JEE Main 2024 · 30 Jan Shift 2 · Q33 · MCQ

Assertion A: $\text{H}_2\text{Te}$ is more acidic than $\text{H}_2\text{S}$.
Reason R: The bond dissociation enthalpy of $\text{H}_2\text{Te}$ is lower than that of $\text{H}_2\text{S}$.

(1) Both true, R is not the explanation  (2) Both true and R explains A  (3) A false, R true  (4) A true, R false

**Answer: (2)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Down the group, the H–E bond lengthens and weakens (Te–H $<$ S–H in bond enthalpy), so $\text{H}_2\text{Te}$ loses $\text{H}^+$ more easily and is the stronger acid ($\text{p}K_a$ of $\text{H}_2\text{Te}\approx2.6$ vs $7.0$ for $\text{H}_2\text{S}$). R explains A.

### C-PBL-09 — JEE Main 2024 · 30 Jan Shift 2 · Q41 · MCQ

Choose the correct statements about the hydrides of group 15 elements.
A. Stability decreases in the order $\text{NH}_3>\text{PH}_3>\text{AsH}_3>\text{SbH}_3>\text{BiH}_3$.
B. Reducing ability increases in the order $\text{NH}_3<\text{PH}_3<\text{AsH}_3<\text{SbH}_3<\text{BiH}_3$.
C. Among the hydrides, $\text{NH}_3$ is a strong reducing agent while $\text{BiH}_3$ is a mild reducing agent.
D. Basicity increases in the order $\text{NH}_3<\text{PH}_3<\text{AsH}_3<\text{SbH}_3<\text{BiH}_3$.

(1) B and C only  (2) C and D only  (3) A and B only  (4) A and D only

**Answer: (3)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Down the group the E–H bond weakens, so stability falls (A true) and reducing power rises (B true). C is the reverse of B, so false. Basicity decreases from $\text{NH}_3$ to $\text{BiH}_3$ (the lone pair becomes more diffuse), so D is false.

### C-PBL-10 — JEE Main 2024 · 31 Jan Shift 1 · Q34 · MCQ

Consider the oxides of group 14 elements $\text{SiO}_2,\ \text{GeO}_2,\ \text{SnO}_2,\ \text{PbO}_2,\ \text{CO}$ and $\text{GeO}$. The amphoteric oxides are

(1) GeO, GeO$_2$  (2) $\text{SiO}_2$, $\text{GeO}_2$  (3) $\text{SnO}_2$, $\text{PbO}_2$  (4) $\text{SnO}_2$, CO

**Answer: (3)** · key ✔

**Solution.** On descending group 14 the oxides change from acidic ($\text{CO}_2,\text{SiO}_2,\text{GeO}_2$) to amphoteric ($\text{SnO}_2,\text{PbO}_2$, which react with both acids and bases). CO is neutral.

### C-PBL-11 — JEE Main 2024 · 31 Jan Shift 1 · Q44 · MCQ

Statement I: Noble gases have very high boiling points.
Statement II: Noble gases are monoatomic gases. They are held together by strong dispersion forces. Because of this they are liquefied at very low temperature. Hence, they have very high boiling points.

(1) I false, II true  (2) Both true  (3) I true, II false  (4) Both false

**Answer: (4)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Noble gases have very *low* boiling points (He $4.2$ K, Ar $87$ K) because the only interatomic forces are *weak* London dispersion forces. Both statements are wrong.

### C-PBL-12 — JEE Main 2024 · 31 Jan Shift 2 · Q37 · MCQ

Statement I: Group 13 trivalent halides get easily hydrolysed by water due to their covalent nature.
Statement II: $\text{AlCl}_3$ upon hydrolysis in acidified aqueous solution forms the octahedral $[\text{Al(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{3+}$ ion.

(1) I true, II false  (2) I false, II true  (3) Both false  (4) Both true

**Answer: (4)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Covalent group 13 halides are Lewis acids with an empty $p$ orbital; water attacks and hydrolyses them. $\text{AlCl}_3$ in acidified water gives the octahedral hexaaqua ion $[\text{Al(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{3+}$. Both statements are true.

### C-PBL-13 — JEE Main 2024 · 1 Feb Shift 1 · Q34 · MCQ

Assertion A: $\text{PH}_3$ has a lower boiling point than $\text{NH}_3$.
Reason R: In the liquid state $\text{NH}_3$ molecules are associated through van der Waals forces, but $\text{PH}_3$ molecules are associated through hydrogen bonding.

(1) Both A and R are correct and R is not the correct explanation of A  (2) A is not correct but R is correct  (3) Both A and R are correct and R is the correct explanation of A  (4) A is correct but R is not correct

**Answer: (4)** · key ✔

**Solution.** $\text{NH}_3$ is strongly hydrogen-bonded (N is small and highly electronegative), which raises its boiling point above that of $\text{PH}_3$; so A is true. The reason has the two molecules swapped: $\text{NH}_3$ shows hydrogen bonding and $\text{PH}_3$ only weak van der Waals forces. R is false.

### C-PBL-14 — JEE Main 2024 · 1 Feb Shift 2 · Q37 · MCQ

Statement I: $\text{SiO}_2$ and $\text{GeO}_2$ are acidic while $\text{SnO}$ and $\text{PbO}$ are amphoteric.
Statement II: Allotropic forms of carbon are due to the property of catenation and $p\pi$–$d\pi$ bond formation.

(1) Both false  (2) Both true  (3) I true, II false  (4) I true, II true

**Answer: (3)** · key ✔

**Solution.** I is true: acidity/basicity trend in group 14 oxides. II is false: carbon's allotropy is due to catenation and $p\pi$–$p\pi$ multiple bonding; carbon has no $d$ orbitals.

### C-PBL-15 — JEE Main 2024 · 1 Feb Shift 2 · Q43 · MCQ

The strongest reducing agent among the following is:

(1) $\text{NH}_3$  (2) $\text{SbH}_3$  (3) $\text{BiH}_3$  (4) $\text{PH}_3$

**Answer: (3)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Down group 15 the $\text{E-H}$ bond weakens and hydrides become less stable, so the reducing power rises: $\text{NH}_3<\text{PH}_3<\text{AsH}_3<\text{SbH}_3<\text{BiH}_3$.

### C-PBL-16 — JEE Main 2024 · 4 Apr Shift 2 · Q44 · MCQ

When $\text{MnO}_2$ and conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ are added to a salt A, a greenish-yellow gas is liberated. Salt A is:

(1) $\text{CaI}_2$  (2) $\text{NaBr}$  (3) $\text{KNO}_3$  (4) $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$

**Answer: (4)** · key ✔

**Solution.** The greenish-yellow gas is $\text{Cl}_2$: $\text{MnO}_2+4\text{HCl}\to\text{MnCl}_2+\text{Cl}_2+2\text{H}_2\text{O}$, where HCl comes from a chloride with conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$. Iodide would give violet $\text{I}_2$, bromide brown $\text{Br}_2$, nitrate brown $\text{NO}_2$.

### C-PBL-17 — JEE Main 2024 · 5 Apr Shift 1 · Q33 · MCQ

Statement I: In group 13, the stability of the $+1$ oxidation state increases down the group.
Statement II: The atomic size of gallium is greater than that of aluminium.

(1) Both correct  (2) I incorrect, II correct  (3) Both incorrect  (4) I correct, II incorrect

**Answer: (4)** · key ✔

**Solution.** The inert-pair effect makes $+1$ progressively more stable from Ga to Tl: I is true. Ga ($135$ pm) is slightly *smaller* than Al ($143$ pm) because of poor shielding by the $3d^{10}$ electrons, so II is false.

### C-PBL-18 — JEE Main 2024 · 5 Apr Shift 1 · Q37 · Numerical

The number of neutrons in the more abundant isotope of boron is $x$. Amorphous boron heated with air forms a product in which the oxidation state of boron is $y$. $x+y=$ ______.

**Answer: 9** · key ✔

**Solution.** The more abundant isotope is $^{11}_{5}\text{B}$ ($80\%$): neutrons $=11-5=6$. Boron burns in air to $\text{B}_2\text{O}_3$ (with some BN), where B is $+3$. $x+y=6+3=9$.

### C-PBL-19 — JEE Main 2024 · 5 Apr Shift 2 · Q36 · MCQ

Correct statements: (A) The decreasing order of atomic radii of group 13 elements is $\text{Tl}>\text{In}>\text{Ga}>\text{Al}>\text{B}$. (B) Down group 13, electronegativity decreases from top to bottom. (C) Al dissolves in dil. HCl and liberates $\text{H}_2$, but conc. $\text{HNO}_3$ renders Al passive by forming a protective oxide layer. (D) All elements of group 13 exhibit a highly stable $+1$ oxidation state. (E) The hybridisation of Al in $[\text{Al(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{3+}$ is $sp^3d^2$.

(1) (A), (C), (E) only  (2) (A), (C) only  (3) (C), (E) only  (4) (A), (B), (C), (E) only

**Answer: (3)** · key ✔

**Solution.** (A) is false: Ga ($135$ pm) is smaller than Al ($143$ pm). (B) is false: electronegativity is not monotonic (Al $1.5$, Ga $1.6$, In $1.7$, Tl $1.8$). (C) is true. (D) is false: $+1$ is stable only for Tl. (E) is true: an octahedral $[\text{Al(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{3+}$ uses $sp^3d^2$. Answer: C and E.
