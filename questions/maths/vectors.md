### M-VEC-01 — JEE Main 2024 · 27 Jan Shift 1 · Q78 · MCQ

Let $\vec a=\hat i+2\hat j+\hat k$, $\vec b=3(\hat i-\hat j+\hat k)$, and let $\vec c$ be a vector with $\vec a\times\vec c=\vec b$ and $\vec a\cdot\vec c=3$. Then $\vec a\cdot\big((\vec c\times\vec b)-\vec b-\vec c\big)$ is equal to

(1) 32  (2) 24  (3) 20  (4) 36

**Answer: (2)** · key ✔

**Solution.** $\vec a\cdot(\vec c\times\vec b)=(\vec a\times\vec c)\cdot\vec b=\vec b\cdot\vec b=9\cdot3=27$. Also $\vec a\cdot\vec b=3(1-2+1)=0$ and $\vec a\cdot\vec c=3$. So the value is $27-0-3=24$.

### M-VEC-02 — JEE Main 2024 · 27 Jan Shift 2 · Q77 · MCQ

The position vectors of the vertices $A,B,C$ of a triangle are $2\hat i-3\hat j+3\hat k$, $2\hat i+2\hat j+3\hat k$ and $-\hat i+\hat j+3\hat k$. Let $l$ be the length of the angle bisector $AD$ of $\angle BAC$, where $D$ lies on $BC$. Then $2l^2$ equals

(1) 49  (2) 42  (3) 50  (4) 45

**Answer: (4)** · key ✔

**Solution.** $AB=\sqrt{0+25+0}=5$ and $AC=\sqrt{9+16+0}=5$, so the triangle is isosceles and the bisector from $A$ meets $BC$ at its midpoint $D=\left(\tfrac12,\tfrac32,3\right)$. $AD^2=\left(\tfrac32\right)^2+\left(\tfrac92\right)^2=\tfrac{9+81}4=22.5$, so $2l^2=45$.

### M-VEC-03 — JEE Main 2024 · 27 Jan Shift 2 · Q78 · MCQ

The position vectors of the vertices of a triangle are $A(2,2,1)$, $B(1,2,2)$ and $C(2,1,2)$. If $l_1,l_2,l_3$ are the lengths of the perpendiculars from the orthocentre of the triangle to the sides $AB,BC,CA$ respectively, then $l_1^2+l_2^2+l_3^2$ equals

(1) $\tfrac15$  (2) $\tfrac12$  (3) $\tfrac14$  (4) $\tfrac13$

**Answer: (2)** · key ✔

**Solution.** $AB=BC=CA=\sqrt2$, so the triangle is equilateral and the orthocentre coincides with the centroid and incentre. Each perpendicular equals the inradius $r=\dfrac{a}{2\sqrt3}=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2\sqrt3}=\dfrac1{\sqrt6}$. So $\sum l_i^2=3\cdot\dfrac16=\dfrac12$.

### M-VEC-04 — JEE Main 2024 · 29 Jan Shift 1 · Q77 · MCQ

Let $\vec a,\vec b,\vec c$ be three non-zero vectors such that $\vec b$ and $\vec c$ are non-collinear. If $\vec a+5\vec b$ is collinear with $\vec c$, $\vec b+6\vec c$ is collinear with $\vec a$, and $\vec a+\alpha\vec b+\beta\vec c=\vec0$, then $\alpha+\beta$ is equal to

(1) 35  (2) 30  (3) $-30$  (4) $-25$

**Answer: (1)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Let $\vec a+5\vec b=\lambda\vec c$, so $\vec a=\lambda\vec c-5\vec b$. Also $\vec b+6\vec c=\mu\vec a=\mu\lambda\vec c-5\mu\vec b$. Comparing coefficients of the independent vectors $\vec b,\vec c$: $1=-5\mu\Rightarrow\mu=-\tfrac15$ and $6=\mu\lambda\Rightarrow\lambda=-30$. Then $\vec a=-30\vec c-5\vec b$, i.e. $\vec a+5\vec b+30\vec c=\vec0$, so $\alpha=5,\ \beta=30$ and $\alpha+\beta=35$.

### M-VEC-05 — JEE Main 2024 · 29 Jan Shift 1 · Q78 · MCQ

Let $O$ be the origin and the position vectors of $A$ and $B$ be $2\hat i+2\hat j+\hat k$ and $2\hat i+4\hat j+4\hat k$. If the internal bisector of $\angle AOB$ meets $AB$ at $C$, then the length of $OC$ is

(1) $\tfrac23\sqrt{31}$  (2) $\tfrac23\sqrt{34}$  (3) $\tfrac34\sqrt{34}$  (4) $\tfrac32\sqrt{31}$

**Answer: (2)** · key ✔

**Solution.** $OA=3$ and $OB=6$. By the angle bisector theorem $AC:CB=OA:OB=1:2$, so $C=\dfrac{2\vec A+\vec B}{3}=\left(2,\tfrac83,2\right)$. $OC^2=4+\tfrac{64}9+4=\tfrac{136}9$, so $OC=\dfrac23\sqrt{34}$.

### M-VEC-06 — JEE Main 2024 · 29 Jan Shift 2 · Q77 · MCQ

Let $\overrightarrow{OA}=\vec a$, $\overrightarrow{OB}=12\vec a+4\vec b$ and $\overrightarrow{OC}=\vec b$, where $O$ is the origin. If $S$ is the parallelogram with adjacent sides $OA$ and $OC$, then $\dfrac{\text{area of quadrilateral }OABC}{\text{area of }S}$ is equal to

(1) 6  (2) 10  (3) 7  (4) 8

**Answer: (4)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Area of $OABC=\text{area}(\triangle OAB)+\text{area}(\triangle OBC)=\tfrac12|\vec a\times(12\vec a+4\vec b)|+\tfrac12|(12\vec a+4\vec b)\times\vec b|=\tfrac12(4)|\vec a\times\vec b|+\tfrac12(12)|\vec a\times\vec b|=8|\vec a\times\vec b|$. Area of $S=|\vec a\times\vec b|$. Ratio $=8$.

### M-VEC-07 — JEE Main 2024 · 30 Jan Shift 1 · Q77 · MCQ

Let $A(2,3,5)$ and $C(-3,4,-2)$ be opposite vertices of a parallelogram $ABCD$. If the diagonal $\overrightarrow{BD}=\hat i+2\hat j+3\hat k$, then the area of the parallelogram is equal to

### M-VEC-08 — JEE Main 2024 · 30 Jan Shift 1 · Q78 · MCQ

Let $\vec a$ and $\vec b$ be vectors with $|\vec a|=1$, $\vec a\cdot\vec b=2$ and $|\vec b|=4$. If $\vec c=2(\vec a\times\vec b)-3\vec b$, then the angle between $\vec b$ and $\vec c$ is

(1) $\cos^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac2{\sqrt3}\right)$  (2) $\cos^{-1}\!\left(-\dfrac1{\sqrt3}\right)$  (3) $\cos^{-1}\!\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt3}2\right)$  (4) $\cos^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac23\right)$

**Answer: (3)** · key ✔

**Solution.** $\vec b\cdot\vec c=2\vec b\cdot(\vec a\times\vec b)-3|\vec b|^2=0-48=-48$. $|\vec a\times\vec b|^2=|\vec a|^2|\vec b|^2-(\vec a\cdot\vec b)^2=16-4=12$, and since $\vec a\times\vec b\perp\vec b$: $|\vec c|^2=4(12)+9(16)=192\Rightarrow|\vec c|=8\sqrt3$. $\cos\theta=\dfrac{-48}{4\cdot8\sqrt3}=-\dfrac{\sqrt3}2$.

### M-VEC-09 — JEE Advanced 2025 · Paper 1 · Q9 · Numerical

$P,Q,R$ are distinct points in the plane and $S$ is inside $\triangle PQR$ with $\overrightarrow{SP}+5\overrightarrow{SQ}+6\overrightarrow{SR}=\vec0$. $E$ and $F$ are the mid-points of $PR$ and $QR$. Then $\dfrac{EF}{ES}=$ ______.

**Answer: 1.2** (accepted range $1.15$–$1.25$) · official key ✔ (re-derived)

**Solution.** From the condition, $S=\dfrac{P+5Q+6R}{12}$. $E=\dfrac{P+R}2$, so $S-E=\dfrac{P+5Q+6R-6P-6R}{12}=\dfrac{5(Q-P)}{12}$, giving $ES=\dfrac5{12}PQ$. $EF=\tfrac12PQ$ (mid-point theorem in $\triangle PQR$: $E,F$ join the mid-points of $PR$ and $QR$). $\dfrac{EF}{ES}=\dfrac{1/2}{5/12}=\dfrac65=1.2$.

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 21 Jan Shift 2 · set Q1 · MCQ

For a triangle $ABC$, let $\vec p=\overrightarrow{BC}$, $\vec q=\overrightarrow{CA}$ and $\vec r=\overrightarrow{BA}$. If $|\vec p|=2\sqrt3$, $|\vec q|=2$ and $\cos\theta=\dfrac1{\sqrt3}$, where $\theta$ is the angle between $\vec p$ and $\vec q$, then $\left|\vec p\times(\vec q-3\vec r)\right|^2+3|\vec r|^2$ is equal to:

(1) 410  (2) 340  (3) 200  (4) 220

**Answer: (3)** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** From the triangle, $\vec r=\overrightarrow{BC}+\overrightarrow{CA}=\vec p+\vec q$. Then $\vec p\times(\vec q-3\vec r)=\vec p\times\vec q-3\vec p\times(\vec p+\vec q)=-2(\vec p\times\vec q)$. $|\vec p\times\vec q|^2=12\cdot4\cdot\sin^2\theta=48\cdot\tfrac23=32$, so the first term is $4\cdot32=128$. $\vec p\cdot\vec q=2\sqrt3\cdot2\cdot\tfrac1{\sqrt3}=4$ and $|\vec r|^2=12+4+2\cdot4=24$, so $3|\vec r|^2=72$. Total $=200$.

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 28 Jan Shift 2 · set Q3 · MCQ

(1) 2  (2) $\dfrac{\sqrt{30}}4$  (3) $\dfrac32$  (4) $\dfrac{\sqrt{26}}4$

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 24 Jan Shift 2 · set Q5 · MCQ

Let $\vec a=2\hat i-\hat j+\hat k$ and $\vec b=\lambda\hat j+2\hat k$, $\lambda\in\mathbb Z$ be two vectors. Let $\vec c=\vec a\times\vec b$ and $\vec d$ be a vector of magnitude 2 in the $yz$-plane. If $|\vec c|=\sqrt{53}$, then the maximum possible value of $(\vec c\cdot\vec d)^2$ is equal to:

(1) 26  (2) 52  (3) 208  (4) 104

**Answer: (2)** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** $\vec c=\vec a\times\vec b=(-2-\lambda)\hat i-4\hat j+2\lambda\hat k$. $|\vec c|^2=(2+\lambda)^2+16+4\lambda^2=53\Rightarrow5\lambda^2+4\lambda-33=0\Rightarrow\lambda=-3$ (the other root $2.2$ is not an integer). So $\vec c=(1,-4,-6)$. For $\vec d=(0,y,z)$ with $y^2+z^2=4$: $(\vec c\cdot\vec d)^2=(4y+6z)^2\le(4^2+6^2)(y^2+z^2)=52\cdot4=208$ (Cauchy–Schwarz, attained when $\vec d\parallel(-4,-6)$ in the $yz$-plane).

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 22 Jan Shift 2 · set Q6 · MCQ

Let a vector $\vec a=\sqrt2\hat i-\hat j+\lambda\hat k$, $\lambda>0$, make an obtuse angle with the vector $\vec b=-\lambda^2\hat i+4\sqrt2\hat j+4\sqrt2\hat k$ and an angle $\theta$, $\dfrac\pi6<\theta<\dfrac\pi2$, with the positive $z$-axis. If the set of all possible values of $\lambda$ is $(\alpha,\beta)-\{\gamma\}$, then $\alpha+\beta+\gamma$ is equal to ______.

**Answer: (3)** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** Angle with the $z$-axis: $\cos\theta=\dfrac\lambda{\sqrt{3+\lambda^2}}$ with $0<\cos\theta<\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\Rightarrow\lambda>0$ and $4\lambda^2<9+3\lambda^2\Rightarrow\lambda<3$. So $\lambda\in(0,3)-\{2\}$ and $\alpha+\beta+\gamma=0+3+2=5$.

**Solution.** $\vec a\times(\vec b-2\vec c)=\vec0$, so $\vec b-2\vec c=k\vec a$. $\vec b\cdot\vec c=4\cdot2\cos60^\circ=4$. $|\vec b-2\vec c|^2=16-4(4)+4(4)=16=k^2|\vec a|^2\Rightarrow k=\pm4$. ### § — JEE Main 2026 · 21 Jan Shift 1 · set Q11 · MCQ

For three unit vectors $\vec a,\vec b,\vec c$ satisfying $|\vec a-\vec b|^2+|\vec b-\vec c|^2+|\vec c-\vec a|^2=9$ and $|2\vec a+k\vec b+k\vec c|=3$, the positive value of $k$ is:

(1) 4  (2) 3  (3) 5  (4) 6

**Answer: (1)** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** $\sum|\vec a-\vec b|^2=6-2(\vec a\cdot\vec b+\vec b\cdot\vec c+\vec c\cdot\vec a)=9$, so $\vec a\cdot\vec b+\vec b\cdot\vec c+\vec c\cdot\vec a=-\tfrac32$. Then $|\vec a+\vec b+\vec c|^2=3+2\left(-\tfrac32\right)=0$, so $\vec b+\vec c=-\vec a$. Hence $|2\vec a+k(\vec b+\vec c)|=|(2-k)\vec a|=|2-k|=3$, and the positive value is $k=5$.
