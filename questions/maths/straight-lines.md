### M-STL-01 — JEE Main 2024 · 27 Jan Shift 1 · Q65 · MCQ

The portion of the line $4x+5y=20$ in the first quadrant is trisected by the lines $L_1$ and $L_2$ passing through the origin. The tangent of an angle between $L_1$ and $L_2$ is

(1) $\tfrac85$  (2) $\tfrac{25}{41}$  (3) $\tfrac25$  (4) $\tfrac{30}{41}$

**Answer: (4)** · key ✔

**Solution.** The segment runs from $(5,0)$ to $(0,4)$, with trisection points $\left(\tfrac{10}3,\tfrac43\right)$ and $\left(\tfrac53,\tfrac83\right)$. Slopes: $m_1=\tfrac25$, $m_2=\tfrac85$. $\tan\theta=\left|\dfrac{m_2-m_1}{1+m_1m_2}\right|=\dfrac{6/5}{1+16/25}=\dfrac{6/5}{41/25}=\dfrac{30}{41}$.

### M-STL-02 — JEE Main 2024 · 27 Jan Shift 2 · Q66 · MCQ

Let $R$ be the interior region between the lines $3x-y+1=0$ and $x+2y-5=0$ containing the origin. The set of all values of $a$ for which the points $(a^2,a+1)$ lie in $R$ is

(1) $(-3,-1)\cup\left(-\tfrac13,1\right)$  (2) $(-3,0)\cup\left(\tfrac13,1\right)$  (3) $(-3,0)\cup\left(\tfrac23,1\right)$  (4) $(-3,-1)\cup\left(\tfrac13,1\right)$

**Answer: (2)** · key ✔

**Solution.** At the origin $3x-y+1=1>0$ and $x+2y-5=-5<0$, so $R:\ 3x-y+1>0,\ x+2y-5<0$. For $(a^2,a+1)$: $3a^2-(a+1)+1=3a^2-a>0\Rightarrow a<0$ or $a>\tfrac13$. And $a^2+2(a+1)-5=a^2+2a-3<0\Rightarrow-3<a<1$. Intersection: $(-3,0)\cup\left(\tfrac13,1\right)$.

### M-STL-03 — JEE Main 2024 · 27 Jan Shift 2 · Q83 · Numerical

If the sum of the squares of all real values of $\alpha$ for which the lines $2x-y+3=0$, $6x+3y+1=0$ and $\alpha x+2y-2=0$ do not form a triangle is $p$, then the greatest integer $\le p$ is ______.

**Answer: 32** · key ✔

**Solution.** Three lines fail to form a triangle if two are parallel or all three are concurrent. Slopes of the first two are $2$ and $-2$. Third parallel to first: $-\tfrac\alpha2=2\Rightarrow\alpha=-4$. Parallel to second: $\alpha=4$. Concurrency: the first two meet at $\left(-\tfrac56,\tfrac43\right)$; substituting in the third: $-\tfrac{5\alpha}6+\tfrac83-2=0\Rightarrow\alpha=\tfrac45$. $p=16+16+\tfrac{16}{25}=32.64$, so $\lfloor p\rfloor=32$.

### M-STL-04 — JEE Main 2024 · 29 Jan Shift 1 · Q66 · MCQ

In a $\triangle ABC$, suppose $y=x$ is the equation of the bisector of the angle $B$ and the equation of the side $AC$ is $2x-y=2$. If $2AB=BC$ and the points $A$ and $B$ are $(4,6)$ and $(\alpha,\beta)$ respectively, then $\alpha+2\beta$ is equal to

(1) $-4$  (2) $42$  (3) $2$  (4) $-1$

**Answer: (2)** · key ✔

**Solution.** The reflection of $A$ in the bisector $y=x$ is $A'(6,4)$, which lies on line $BC$ with $BA'=BA$. Let $B=(t,t)$. Since $BC=2BA=2BA'$ and $A'$ lies on $BC$: $C=2A'-B=(12-t,\,8-t)$. $C$ lies on $2x-y=2$: $2(12-t)-(8-t)=2\Rightarrow t=14$. So $B=(14,14)$ and $\alpha+2\beta=14+28=42$.

### M-STL-05 — JEE Main 2024 · 29 Jan Shift 2 · Q66 · MCQ

Let $A$ be the point of intersection of the lines $4x+3y=8$ and $6x+y=5$, and $B$ the point of intersection of the lines $3x+2y=14$ and $5x-y=6$. The distance of the point $P(5,-2)$ from the line $AB$ is

(1) $\tfrac{13}2$  (2) 8  (3) $\tfrac52$  (4) 6

**Answer: (4)** · key ✔

**Solution.** $A$: from $y=5-6x$, $4x+15-18x=8\Rightarrow x=\tfrac12,\ y=2$. $B$: from $y=5x-6$, $3x+10x-12=14\Rightarrow x=2,\ y=4$. Line $AB$: slope $\tfrac{2}{3/2}=\tfrac43$, i.e. $4x-3y+4=0$. Distance of $P$: $\dfrac{|20+6+4|}{5}=6$.

### M-STL-06 — JEE Main 2024 · 29 Jan Shift 2 · Q67 · MCQ

The distance of the point $(2,3)$ from the line $2x-3y+28=0$, measured parallel to the line $\sqrt3x-y+1=0$, is equal to

(1) $4\sqrt2$  (2) $6\sqrt3$  (3) $3+4\sqrt2$  (4) $4+6\sqrt3$

**Answer: (4)** · key ✔

**Solution.** The given direction has slope $\sqrt3$ (angle $60^\circ$). Points at distance $t$ along it: $\left(2+\tfrac t2,\ 3+\tfrac{\sqrt3}2t\right)$. Substituting in $2x-3y+28=0$: $4+t-9-\tfrac{3\sqrt3}2t+28=0\Rightarrow t=\dfrac{23}{\frac{3\sqrt3}2-1}=\dfrac{46}{3\sqrt3-2}=\dfrac{46(3\sqrt3+2)}{23}=6\sqrt3+4$.

### M-STL-07 — JEE Main 2024 · 30 Jan Shift 1 · Q64 · MCQ

A line passing through the point $A(9,0)$ makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the positive direction of the $x$-axis. If this line is rotated about $A$ through an angle of $15^\circ$ in the clockwise direction, then its equation in the new position is

(1) $\dfrac y{\sqrt3-2}+x=9$  (2) $\dfrac x{\sqrt3-2}+y=9$  (3) $\dfrac x{\sqrt3+2}+y=9$  (4) $\dfrac y{\sqrt3+2}+x=9$

**Answer: (1)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Clockwise rotation reduces the inclination to $30^\circ-15^\circ=15^\circ$, with slope $\tan15^\circ=2-\sqrt3$. The line is $y=(2-\sqrt3)(x-9)$, i.e. $y=(\sqrt3-2)(9-x)\Rightarrow\dfrac y{\sqrt3-2}+x=9$.

### M-STL-08 — JEE Main 2024 · 30 Jan Shift 2 · Q65 · MCQ

If $x^2-y^2+2hxy+2gx+2fy+c=0$ is the locus of a point which moves so that it is always equidistant from the lines $x+2y+7=0$ and $2x-y+8=0$, then $g+c+h-f$ equals

(1) 14  (2) 6  (3) 8  (4) 29

**Answer: (1)** · key ✔

**Solution.** Equal distances: $(x+2y+7)^2=(2x-y+8)^2$ (both denominators are $\sqrt5$), i.e. $(-x+3y-1)(3x+y+15)=0$. Expanding: $-3x^2+8xy+3y^2-18x+44y-15=0$. Dividing by $-3$: $x^2-\tfrac83xy-y^2+6x-\tfrac{44}3y+5=0$. So $h=-\tfrac43,\ g=3,\ f=-\tfrac{22}3,\ c=5$, and $g+c+h-f=3+5-\tfrac43+\tfrac{22}3=8+6=14$.

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 23 Jan Shift 1 · set Q1 · MCQ

A rectangle is formed by the lines $x=0$, $y=0$, $x=3$ and $y=4$. Let the line $L$ be perpendicular to $3x+y+6=0$ and divide the area of the rectangle into two equal parts. Then the distance of the point $\left(\dfrac12,-5\right)$ from the line $L$ is equal to:

(1) $\sqrt{10}$  (2) $2\sqrt5$  (3) $2\sqrt{10}$  (4) $3\sqrt{10}$

**Answer: (3)** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** A line through the centre of a rectangle bisects its area, so $L$ passes through $\left(\tfrac32,2\right)$. The slope of $3x+y+6=0$ is $-3$, so $L$ has slope $\tfrac13$: $y-2=\tfrac13\left(x-\tfrac32\right)\Rightarrow2x-6y+9=0$. Distance of $\left(\tfrac12,-5\right)$: $\dfrac{|1+30+9|}{\sqrt{4+36}}=\dfrac{40}{2\sqrt{10}}=2\sqrt{10}$.

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 24 Jan Shift 2 · set Q2 · MCQ

Let the angles made with the positive $x$-axis by two straight lines drawn from the point $P(2,3)$ and meeting the line $x+y=6$ at a distance $\sqrt{\dfrac23}$ from the point $P$ be $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$. Then the value of $(\theta_1+\theta_2)$ is:

(1) $\dfrac\pi3$  (2) $\dfrac\pi6$  (3) $\dfrac\pi2$  (4) $\dfrac\pi{12}$

**Answer: (3)** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** Points at distance $r=\sqrt{2/3}$ from $P$ in direction $\theta$ are $(2+r\cos\theta,\,3+r\sin\theta)$. On $x+y=6$: $r(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)=1\Rightarrow\cos\theta+\sin\theta=\sqrt{\tfrac32}\Rightarrow\sin\left(\theta+\tfrac\pi4\right)=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$. So $\theta+\tfrac\pi4=\tfrac\pi3$ or $\tfrac{2\pi}3$, giving $\theta_1=\tfrac\pi{12}$, $\theta_2=\tfrac{5\pi}{12}$ and $\theta_1+\theta_2=\tfrac\pi2$.

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 22 Jan Shift 2 · set Q3 · MCQ

Among the statements (S1): If $A(5,-1)$ and $B(-2,3)$ are two vertices of a triangle whose orthocentre is $(0,0)$, then its third vertex is $(-4,-7)$; and (S2): If positive numbers $2a,b,c$ are three consecutive terms of an A.P., then the lines $ax+by+c=0$ are concurrent at $(2,-2)$:

(1) both are incorrect  (2) only (S2) is correct  (3) only (S1) is correct  (4) both are correct

**Answer: (4)** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** S1: let $C=(h,k)$. $AO\perp BC$: slope of $AO=-\tfrac15$ so slope of $BC=5$, i.e. $k-3=5(h+2)\Rightarrow5h-k+13=0$. $BO\perp AC$: slope of $BO=-\tfrac32$ so slope of $AC=\tfrac23$, i.e. $3(k+1)=2(h-5)\Rightarrow2h-3k-13=0$. Solving: $h=-4$, $k=-7$ – correct. S2: $2a,b,c$ in A.P. means $2b=2a+c$, i.e. $2a-2b+c=0$; this says $(x,y)=(2,-2)$ satisfies $ax+by+c=0$ for every such $(a,b,c)$, so the lines are concurrent at $(2,-2)$ – correct.

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 23 Jan Shift 2 · set Q4 · MCQ

Let $A(1,2)$ and $C(-3,-6)$ be two diagonally opposite vertices of a rhombus, whose sides AD and BC are parallel to the line $7x-y=14$. If $B(\alpha,\beta)$ and $D(\gamma,\delta)$ are the other two vertices, then $|\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta|$ is equal to:

(1) 6  (2) 9  (3) 3  (4) 1

**Answer: (1)** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other, so the midpoint of $BD$ is the midpoint of $AC$, $(-1,-2)$. Hence $\alpha+\gamma=-2$ and $\beta+\delta=-4$, and $|\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta|=6$. (The line $7x-y=14$ fixes the actual positions of B and D, but the sum does not depend on it.)

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 21 Jan Shift 1 · set Q5 · MCQ

Let a point $A$ lie between the parallel lines $L_1$ and $L_2$ such that its distances from $L_1$ and $L_2$ are 6 and 3 units, respectively. Then the area (in sq. units) of the equilateral triangle $ABC$, where the points $B$ and $C$ lie on the lines $L_1$ and $L_2$, respectively, is:

(1) $15\sqrt6$  (2) $21\sqrt3$  (3) $12\sqrt2$  (4) 27

**Answer: (2)** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** Take $L_2:y=0$, $L_1:y=9$ and $A=(0,3)$. Then $\vec{AB}=(x_B,6)$ and $\vec{AC}=(x_C,-3)$. For an equilateral triangle $\vec{AC}$ is $\vec{AB}$ rotated by $60^\circ$: $\left(\tfrac{x_B}2-3\sqrt3,\ \tfrac{\sqrt3}2x_B+3\right)=(x_C,-3)$, so $x_B=-4\sqrt3$ (and $x_C=-5\sqrt3$). Side$^2=x_B^2+36=48+36=84$ and the area is $\dfrac{\sqrt3}4\cdot84=21\sqrt3$.

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 28 Jan Shift 1 · set Q6 · MCQ

Let $ABC$ be an equilateral triangle with orthocentre at the origin and the side $BC$ on the line $x+2\sqrt2y=4$. If the co-ordinates of the vertex $A$ are $(\alpha,\beta)$, then the greatest integer less than or equal to $|\alpha+\sqrt2\beta|$ is:

(1) 2  (2) 5  (3) 3  (4) 4

**Answer: (4)** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** In an equilateral triangle the orthocentre is also the centroid, so $O$ is at distance $r=\dfrac4{\sqrt{1+8}}=\dfrac43$ from $BC$ and $OA=2r=\dfrac83$, with $A$ on the opposite side of $O$ from $BC$. The foot of the perpendicular from $O$ to $BC$ is $D=\dfrac43\cdot\dfrac{(1,2\sqrt2)}3=\left(\tfrac49,\tfrac{8\sqrt2}9\right)$, and $A=-2D=\left(-\tfrac89,-\tfrac{16\sqrt2}9\right)$. Then $\alpha+\sqrt2\beta=-\tfrac89-\tfrac{32}9=-\tfrac{40}9$, so $|\alpha+\sqrt2\beta|=4.44\ldots$ and the greatest integer is 4.

### § — JEE Main 2026 · 24 Jan Shift 1 · set Q7 · MCQ

Let $A(1,0)$, $B(2,-1)$ and $C\left(\dfrac73,\dfrac43\right)$ be three points. If the equation of the bisector of the angle $ABC$ is $\alpha x+\beta y=5$, then the value of $\alpha^2+\beta^2$ is:

(1) 13  (2) 10  (3) 5  (4) 8

**Answer: (2)** · key (compiled)

**Solution.** $\vec{BA}=(-1,1)$ with $|BA|=\sqrt2$, and $\vec{BC}=\left(\tfrac13,\tfrac73\right)$ with $|BC|=\tfrac{5\sqrt2}3$. The bisector direction is the sum of the unit vectors: $\dfrac{(-1,1)}{\sqrt2}+\dfrac{(1,7)}{5\sqrt2}=\dfrac{(-4,12)}{5\sqrt2}\propto(-1,3)$. The bisector through $B(2,-1)$: $3(x-2)+(y+1)=0\Rightarrow3x+y=5$. So $\alpha=3$, $\beta=1$ and $\alpha^2+\beta^2=10$.
