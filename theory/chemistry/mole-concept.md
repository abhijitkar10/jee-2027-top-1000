# Mole Concept & Stoichiometry — theory

> Questions: [../../questions/chemistry/mole-concept.md](../../questions/chemistry/mole-concept.md)

## Key ideas
- $n=\dfrac{m}{M}=\dfrac{N}{N_A}=\dfrac{V_{STP}}{22.4\,\text{L}}$. Limiting reagent = smallest (moles/coefficient). Percentage yield $=\dfrac{\text{actual}}{\text{theoretical}}\times100$.
- Empirical formula from % composition: divide by atomic mass, then by the smallest. Combustion: CO$_2$ gives C, H$_2$O gives H.
- **Concentration:** $M=\dfrac{n}{V(L)}$, $m=\dfrac{n}{\text{kg solvent}}$; $m\leftrightarrow M$: take 1 kg solvent, use density. $N=n\times$ n-factor. Dilution $M_1V_1=M_2V_2$.
- **Titration/n-factor:** $\text{KMnO}_4$ (acid) 5; $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ 6; oxalic acid 2. Equivalents react equally.
- Kjeldahl: $\%N=\dfrac{1.4\,N\,V\,(\text{acid})}{m}$ (not for azo, nitro, ring N as in pyridine).
- Primary standards (Na$_2$CO$_3$, oxalic acid, KHP): pure, stable, non-hygroscopic. NaOH and KMnO$_4$ are secondary.
## Common traps
- Count moles of *ions* separately. Gas volumes of gaseous reactants/products are in mole ratio at the same T, P.
